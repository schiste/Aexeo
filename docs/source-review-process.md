# Source review process

An `evidence_review` record separates two questions: whether the supplied passage supports a particular claim, and whether the source is suitable for that claim. An automated support judgment cannot decide source reliability. Aexeo accepts an external assessment as trusted input; the application using it is responsible for the review process and access to the records.

## Assess a source for a specific claim

1. Preserve the exact claim, the source passage and enough context to interpret it. Identify the full publication, author or issuing organization, publication date, and a stable reference to the version reviewed. Check that the passage is complete enough to support the intended claim, including qualifications, dates and scope. If context is missing, use `uncertain`.
2. Assess the source's suitability for this claim and domain. Record relevant expertise and editorial or institutional oversight, the methods and evidence used, potential conflicts of interest, and whether the source is current enough for the claim. Distinguish primary observations from a source's interpretation. A credible publisher alone does not make every assertion reliable.
3. Check for corrections, retractions, material contradictions and important limitations using sources appropriate to the topic. A lack of information is not proof of reliability. Consider whether the claim calls for specialist review or stronger evidence, especially for health, legal, safety or financial content.
4. Assign `reliable` only when the review has sufficient evidence for this particular claim and use; assign `unreliable` when there is evidence the source is unsuitable; otherwise assign `uncertain`. Record the reviewer, date, decision, reasons, checked references and unresolved limitations. Record conflicts of interest and request an independent reviewer where the risk or organization policy calls for one.
5. Assess support separately against the exact source snapshot: `supported`, `contradicted`, or `uncertain`. An unsupported inference, missing context or disagreement that cannot be resolved should remain `uncertain`; do not infer support from source reputation. Capture the rationale and any independent adjudication in the review system.
6. Store the assessment in an access-controlled, auditable system alongside the snapshot and decision record. Produce the Aexeo review only from an authorized assessment. Aexeo does not authenticate the reviewer field or establish the integrity of stored records; an application requiring those properties must provide them externally.
7. Reassess when the claim, source version, relevant evidence, review policy or intended use changes. Re-evaluate samples and reviewer disagreements over time; track false approvals and unresolved cases rather than treating the number of approvals as a quality measure.

The schema's `reliability` is a claim-specific editorial judgment, not a permanent reputation score for a source. Aexeo binds a review to the exact supplied claim and source text; any change to either makes the old review unavailable for that gate. Changes to external context or a review policy require the application to invalidate or replace the record, because the engine cannot detect them automatically. Keep unresolved reviews as `uncertain` and route them to a human; a passed gate is not proof of factual truth.

This process is a starting procedure, not an audited guarantee. Adapt it to the content, jurisdiction, risk and editorial standards, then validate it with qualified reviewers and representative cases. The Jev adapter's `--source-reviewer` and `--source-review-reason` capture only a small part of this process; they do not validate the assessment or establish reviewer independence.
